# Achievements, challenges and progress plan (G3: design and planning only)

Status: PLANNING ONLY, written 2026-10-11 for Work's assignment (`65103e2`), corrected after Work's review (`b98876f`). Nothing here is built, migrated, enabled or shown to users. It authorizes no code, schema, migration, dependency, points UI, activation, merchant system, live change, deployment, merge or cost. **Every threshold, name, window and wording below is a PROPOSAL for later owner content review, not an approval and not a live feature.** It builds on the accepted G2 semantics in `VIP_REWARDS_PLAN.md` (no cooldown; four rewarded visits per user/location/local day; independent lifetime and spendable outcomes; held visits; reversals; deletion). Production rewards stay DISABLED, so no achievement can exist in production until its own gates are met. The nine Phase 3A decisions (session/MFA authority, independent adjudication, preservation of moderated originals, severing identity) remain binding.

## 1. Principles
- **Real data only.** Progress and awards are computed exclusively from real lifetime-ledger rows. Nothing is invented, sampled, pre-filled or estimated.
- **Lifetime is the source, not spendable.** Achievements read only `lifetime` ledger entries (at most one award per user/location/local day, by the G2 unique index). The spendable balance and cap never affect them, and spending (none exists) cannot reduce an achievement.
- **No streak pressure**, no loss-aversion timers, no public profiles or leaderboards.
- **No offers, merchants or money.** Any attached benefit would trigger the economics/legal review in `VIP_REWARDS_PLAN.md` section 6.
- **Reuse the architecture.** Postgres/Supabase functions, existing Expo/React Native screens and the existing `Icon` set. No new services, schedulers, licenses, fonts, art or photos.
- **Contributor badges are DEFERRED** (not in this specification and not in the catalog); approved-contribution data is not read.

## 2. Data sources and dependency map
Source: `reward_ledger` rows with `kind = 'lifetime'` (awards and reversals), joined to the visit's stored location, `policy_day_local` and `issuance_day_utc`. Held, confirmed-fraud and recorded-only visits contribute nothing; a held visit contributes after clearance, attributed to its ORIGINAL local date (G2 11.4a).
Dependencies: G2 (`record_visit`, ledger, unique lifetime index, reversals, held clearance, stored days, purge, reporting basis) -> G3 evaluation, event log and progress read -> dashboard progress card. Nothing in G3 feeds back into rewards eligibility.

## 3. Catalog: identity, versions and metrics (all proposals)
**Stable identity versus immutable versions.** Each achievement has a stable logical `achievement_key` (for example `explorer_month`). Definitions are immutable rows `(achievement_key, version)` with `effective_from_date` (a local date), metric, threshold, window rule and copy; a change inserts a new version row, never edits one. Events reference `(achievement_key, period_key)` as identity and record the `version` they were decided under, so a threshold change never creates a duplicate badge.
**Metrics, defined separately (no fixed conversions).** Point values are configurable, so no catalog text may say that N points equals M visits.
| Metric | Exact definition | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| `lifetime_points_total` | sum of lifetime ledger amounts (awards plus reversals) | all time |
| `distinct_locations` | count of distinct locations having at least one non-reversed lifetime award | all time or a window |
| `first_daily_places` | count of non-reversed lifetime awards (each is one first-daily check-in at one location on one local day) | a window |
| `distinct_active_days` | count of distinct location-local dates with at least one non-reversed lifetime award (several locations on one date count once) | a window |
Catalog proposals: lifetime badges *First Stop* (`lifetime_points_total` >= 1 award), *Regular* (`lifetime_points_total` >= 50), *Century* (>= 100), *Trailblazer* (`distinct_locations` >= 10); monthly challenges *Explorer of the Month* (`first_daily_places` >= 5 in the calendar month) and *Steady Month* (`distinct_active_days` >= 8); one seasonal collectible (for example a winter season defined by two local dates and a `first_daily_places` target). Dormant by design: a distinct-towns badge until real data exists in more than one town. User value versus effort (candid): the value is acknowledgement and a small reason to return, and retention benefit is unvalidated; the cheapest validation is one lifetime badge plus one monthly challenge; effort is S/M for the catalog, evaluator and progress read, M for the dashboard card and M for seasonal content.

## 4. Version selection and historical windows (deterministic)
- **Windowed challenge or season:** the version used is the one with the greatest `effective_from_date` <= the window's `starts_on`, fixed when the window opens. A version never takes effect inside an open window, and historical windows are never silently re-judged by a newer threshold.
- **Lifetime badge (no window):** the version used for an award decision is the latest in effect at evaluation time. Once awarded under version v the award stands under v: raising the threshold later never revokes it; recalculation after a reversal uses v, the version recorded on the award. Lowering a threshold applies prospectively: a user who meets the new lower threshold is awarded at the next evaluation after it takes effect (award date = that evaluation, never backdated).
- **Window membership** is the visit's stored location-local date, by the location's IANA zone, against the window's local `starts_on`/`ends_on`. A window closes for NEW visits when its end date passes, but the qualifying set of a closed window can still change through late clearance of a held visit or a reversal.
- **Single rule that unifies the cases:** after each evaluation the engine recomputes eligibility for each `(key, period_key)` from the current qualifying set under the selected version, and appends an event ONLY when that eligibility differs from the latest event's state. This one rule produces, deterministically and without duplicates:
  1. **first delayed award:** no prior event, the set now meets the threshold -> `award` (`reason = first_award`, `period_key` = the original window, `created_at` = now, shown as earned late);
  2. **recalculation or revocation of an old award:** latest event is `award`, the set no longer meets the threshold (for example a reversal) -> `revoke` referencing the reversal entry and its decision;
  3. **re-earning after revocation:** latest event is `revoke`, the set meets the threshold again through legitimate late events (a held visit cleared later, or visits inside an open window) -> a new `award` (`reason = re_earn`).
  There is no separate prohibition on closed windows: a closed window can only regain eligibility through legitimately delayed events dated inside it. Unresolved product behavior, stated plainly: whether a very late award (for example cleared weeks after the window) should be shown with an "earned late" label or suppressed from the headline view is a copy/content choice for later owner content review; the engine's default is to show it with the label.
- **Event sequence allocation:** `seq = max(seq) + 1` for `(user_id, achievement_key, period_key)`, computed only while holding the per-user advisory lock (G2 lock order 1), with `unique (user_id, achievement_key, period_key, seq)` as the backstop; every writer (visit, clearance, reversal, repair, purge) takes that same lock first.
- **Worked scenarios (also tests):**
  - *Held October visit cleared in November.* Explorer of the Month v1 threshold 5. The user has 4 qualifying October places; the 5th visit on Oct 30 is held and cleared on Nov 3. Window October, version selected by window start (v1): the set becomes 5 -> one `award` event (`2026-10`, seq 1, v1, created Nov 3, "earned late"). The November window is unaffected (the visit counts on Oct 30 only).
  - *Reversal after month-end.* On Nov 10 an independent MFA admin reverses one of the five October lifetime awards. Recalculation for `2026-10` under v1 gives 4 -> one `revoke` event (seq 2) referencing the reversal entry and decision; the seq 1 award is untouched. If another held October visit is cleared on Nov 20 the set is 5 again -> `award` (seq 3, `re_earn`). Replaying the evaluation appends nothing.
  - *Catalog threshold change.* v2 (threshold 6) takes effect 2026-11-01. October keeps v1 (so the two scenarios above are unchanged); November uses v2. For the lifetime badge Regular, v1 = 50 and v2 = 60: a user awarded under v1 keeps it; a user at 55 who was not yet awarded sees 55 of 60 and gets nothing silently; lowering instead of raising awards prospectively as above.

## 5. Evaluation triggers, catch-up and freshness
- **Triggers.** `evaluate_achievements(user)` runs after the reward in the visit transaction (a third, disjoint savepoint block A after blocks L and S, G2 section 4a) AND at the end of every committed `award_held_visit` clearance and `reverse_reward`, each in its own savepoint block. Achievements are eventually consistent and can never block, roll back or alter a reward, a clearance or a reversal.
- **Failure classes.** Expected, RECOVERABLE achievement failures are caught by an explicit SQLSTATE list (never `when others`): lock not available (`55P03`), statement timeout (`57014`), and a private `OSA01` for an invalid or missing catalog row. Transaction-integrity and security failures PROPAGATE and abort the whole transaction: privilege errors (`42501`), integrity violations other than the designed `on conflict` no-ops (class `23`), data-corruption classes (`XX`), and any identity or authorization mismatch (private `OSA02`).
- **Logging survives the failed savepoint.** The exception handler runs in the outer transaction AFTER the inner block's writes are rolled back, and there it upserts a row in `achievement_eval_queue(user_id primary key, reason, first_failed_at, attempts, last_sqlstate)`. That queue row commits with the visit, clearance or reversal; if the outer transaction itself fails, nothing persists, which is correct because the reward did not either.
- **Bounded idempotent repair, existing infrastructure only, no new scheduler or paid service.** (1) The authenticated progress read (`get_my_progress()`) first checks whether the caller has a queue row (or a stale marker, below) and, if so, runs `evaluate_achievements(auth.uid())` inline: one user, indexed ledger reads, then clears the queue row. So a user who opens their progress converges with no further visit. (2) An admin-gated `repair_achievements(p_limit)` (existing admin identity and MFA, no adjudication power, no client-supplied amounts) drains at most `p_limit` queue rows with `for update skip locked`. (3) Any external cron the project may already have is optional and out of scope.
- **Authoritative current eligibility and safe stale wording.** The progress read always computes `eligible_now` from the ledger and compares it with the latest event. It shows a badge as EARNED only when the latest event is `award` AND `eligible_now` is true. If the latest event is `award` but `eligible_now` is false (a reversal just happened), it shows "Being updated. This badge is under review and may change" and triggers repair; it never shows an ineligible badge as earned. If `eligible_now` is true but no award exists yet, it shows the progress as complete with "Updating" and triggers repair; it never shows an unearned badge as earned.
- **Scenarios (tests).** Failure then read/repair with no further visit (inject `55P03`, queue row exists, the visit and reward are committed, `get_my_progress()` repairs and clears the queue). Concurrent evaluation and reversal (both take the user lock; whichever is second sees the other's committed state; final state equals a single serial run; reversal's own post-commit evaluation produces the revoke). Replay (re-running the same evaluation appends no event). A propagating failure (forced `42501`) aborts the whole transaction and leaves no visit, award or event.

## 6. Authorization, provenance and deletion relationships
- **Internal vs public.** `evaluate_achievements(user)` and the queue repair helpers live in a schema not exposed through PostgREST, with `EXECUTE` revoked from `public`, `anon`, `authenticated` and `service_role`, callable only by the security-definer functions above. The only client read is `get_my_progress()`, which takes NO user parameter and derives identity from `auth.uid()`; for a signed-out caller it raises `28000`. All achievement tables have RLS enabled, no client grants and no client policies, so no client can read or write another user's rows or any row directly.
- **Adjudication authority is unchanged.** Revocation only reflects a ledger reversal already ordered through `reverse_reward` (existing independent MFA admin, no self-adjudication, immutable decision). The engine never adjudicates. `repair_achievements` is admin-gated and never changes ledger data.
- **Event provenance without orphans.** `achievement_events` rows keep `basis_entry_id` and `decision_id` ONLY on `revoke` events (the reversal ledger entry and the moderation decision); `award` events store numeric snapshots (qualifying value and threshold, version) and NO ledger or visit ids, so unmoderated awards depend on no personal history. FKs to the ledger and moderation tables are `ON DELETE RESTRICT` (never cascade), so a purge can never silently orphan or erase an event.
- **Retained dependency closure on account deletion.** Retained set: every `revoke` event and the `award` events of the same `(key, period)` chain (the revoke/award pairs). Their dependencies are the reversal ledger entry (moderated, already retained by G2), its reversed original (retained by the G2 pair rule) and the moderation decision (never purged); nothing else is pulled in. All other events (unmoderated personal events) are DELETED and no extra visit or ledger history is retained for them. Retained events get `user_id` set to NULL (severed). `achievement_events.user_id` is `ON DELETE RESTRICT`, so `delete_my_account` must run the purge first; a test asserts no event still references the user. This is severing, not anonymity, and not a legal-compliance claim.
- **Privileges and trigger.** `reward_purge_owner` (G2 section 7) gets DELETE on `achievement_events` and column UPDATE on `(user_id)` only. The invoker-security guard trigger permits a DELETE only when `current_user = 'reward_purge_owner'` AND the row's chain has no `revoke` event, and permits an UPDATE only when `user_id` goes to NULL with every other column unchanged.
- **Races.** The purge takes the per-user lock before its row locks, like evaluation and reversal. Deletion racing evaluation or reversal ends in one of two states: they commit first and the new events are part of the chain the purge classifies after locking, or the purge commits first and the later call finds no user and is a no-op (a reversal of a deleted account's original is refused per G2). A repeated purge is a no-op.
- **Tests.** Cross-user read/write refusal (another user's id as a parameter is impossible; direct table access refused for `anon` and `authenticated`); internal-function `EXECUTE` refusal; the closure and FK behavior above; purge of a chain with and without revocations; deletion racing reversal and evaluation; repeated purge; the severed rows keep only audit fields.

## 7. Windows and time zones
A window is a pair of LOCAL dates; a qualifying award counts when the visit's stored location-local date is in range, using the location's IANA zone (Cody: America/Denver), so a window never depends on the device clock or the user's own zone. A completed monthly challenge is awarded once per `period_key` (state changes follow section 4). Fixtures: last and first second of a month in America/Denver; DST days (2026-11-01 fall-back, 2027-03-14 spring-forward); a second zone; 23:59 local on the last day versus 00:01 the next (different windows even when the UTC date matches).

## 8. Accessible progress text, labels and states (proposals)
- **One progress element, spoken once.** Each challenge or badge with a numeric target is ONE element with `role="progressbar"` (React Native `accessibilityRole="progressbar"` with `accessibilityValue` min/max/now/text). Its accessible name is the achievement name; its value text is the full sentence, for example "3 of 5 places this month, 2 to go". The visible sentence is the same string drawn as text and is hidden from assistive technology (`aria-hidden` / `importantForAccessibility="no-hide-descendants"`) so it is not spoken twice; the visual bar is part of the same element, not a separate decoration claim. Meaning is never color-only; targets 48 px; fits 200 percent text and 320 px. Device behavior of this pattern must be checked with VoiceOver and TalkBack before acceptance (browser tests never count as reader evidence).
- **Badge cards:** name, state ("Earned 12 Oct 2026", "Earned late for October 2026", "Not earned yet"), and how to earn it written from the metric ("Reach 50 lifetime points", "Check in first-of-the-day at 5 different restrooms in a month"), with no visit/point conversion. A revoked badge says "Removed after a review" with no detail; a stale one says "Being updated. This badge is under review and may change."
- **States, distinguished truthfully.** (a) *Rewards disabled or not live:* keep the honest preview "VIP progress — rewards coming soon"; no numbers. (b) *Signed out:* "Sign in to track your progress." (c) *Enabled, signed in, evaluated:* real numbers, including a real zero ("0 of 5 places this month"), because the server computed it from the ledger for this user; enrollment is simply rewards being enabled plus an authenticated account, with no separate opt-in. (d) *Unavailable* (read error or evaluation not completed): "Progress is unavailable right now. Try again soon", never a zero. Zero is only ever shown in state (c); no sample data, no fake bars, no preview of badges the user cannot earn.
- A polite live region announces a newly earned badge; any celebration respects reduced motion and is optional. Placement: the dashboard VIP card becomes a compact progress card linking to a progress list in the existing account area; no new tab; no public history or other-user data.

## 9. Future local test plan (planning only; no results exist or are claimed)
Disposable local Postgres plus pure table-driven tests, and the existing Chromium suites with the local mock for the UI, only after Work authorizes implementation:
1. Catalog and metrics: each metric computed exactly as in section 3; threshold crossing and off-by-one; no conversion claims in copy; distinct-location, active-day and reversal handling; no streak logic anywhere.
2. Version selection: window version fixed by window start; lifetime award stands under its version; raising and lowering thresholds; the three section 4 scenarios; no duplicate badges; unique `seq` under concurrent sessions.
3. Event rule: first delayed award, revoke, re-earn, replay appends nothing.
4. Triggers, failure classes and freshness: failure then read/repair with no further visit; queue row survives the failed savepoint; recoverable versus propagating SQLSTATEs; concurrent evaluation and reversal; stale-state display (never shows ineligible as earned).
5. Isolation from rewards: injected block A failure leaves the visit and both reward kinds committed; rewards disabled issues no progress.
6. Windows and zones per section 7, including a held visit cleared in a later month.
7. Authorization and deletion per section 6: cross-user and direct-table refusal, internal `EXECUTE` refusal, closure, FK `RESTRICT`, deletion racing reversal/evaluation, repeated purge.
8. No fabricated state: no ledger rows -> no events or badges; state (c) zero only after a real evaluation; state (d) never shows zero.
9. Accessibility: one `progressbar` per target with matching name/value text and no duplicate spoken sentence, no color-only meaning, 48 px, 320 px, enlarged text, live region, reduced motion; axe in Chromium plus the manual VoiceOver/TalkBack checklist.
10. Safety: no client write access to any achievement table, client cannot supply a threshold, window or award, and no new dependency, asset, scheduler or service is introduced.
