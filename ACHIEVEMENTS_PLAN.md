# Achievements, challenges and progress plan (G3: design and planning only)

Status: PLANNING ONLY, written 2026-10-11 for Work's assignment (`65103e2`), corrected after Work's review (`b98876f`). Nothing here is built, migrated, enabled or shown to users. It authorizes no code, schema, migration, dependency, points UI, activation, merchant system, live change, deployment, merge or cost. **Every threshold, name, window and wording below is a PROPOSAL for later owner content review, not an approval and not a live feature.** It builds on the accepted G2 semantics in `VIP_REWARDS_PLAN.md` (no cooldown; four rewarded visits per user/location/local day; independent lifetime and spendable outcomes; held visits; reversals; deletion). Production rewards stay DISABLED, so no achievement can exist in production until its own gates are met. The nine Phase 3A decisions (session/MFA authority, independent adjudication, preservation of moderated originals, severing identity) remain binding.

**Implementation status (2026-10-31, Claude-run, local disposable PostgreSQL 16 only):** the engine, progress read, repair and purge extension of sections 2-7 and 9 are implemented in `supabase/rewards-wip/R007`-`R009` and delivered for Work's review (see `HANDOFF.md`, `TESTING.md`, `docs/evidence/rewards/README.md`). Nothing is enabled or seeded; every definition in tests is synthetic. Decisions made inside the released boundary (each is an implementation choice, flagged for Work): chains are serialized by per-chain advisory locks instead of `FOR UPDATE` (so the writer needs no UPDATE on chains); `get_my_progress` lists the current windows plus every chain that has an event (past windows with no event are not listed); a revoked chain reports state `removed` and still carries its value and threshold so the climb back is visible; `repair_achievements` clamps `p_limit` above 20 to 20 and rejects NULL/below 1 with `22023`; a fixed season is identified by its start date as `period_key`; the evaluation date for lifetime version selection is the UTC date. Corrections after Work's `7c58095` and `f930574` reviews: one request reads at most 5,000 raw award rows (a single cached pass per window, shared by read-repair and display; the over-budget probe is charged); a window larger than the budget is computed EXACTLY across bounded steps by a private resumable calculation state and stays pending/unavailable until finished (never approximated, never stale); a key keeps one window rule; a new overflow rewinds an in-progress re-scan. Section 8 (accessible UI text) is not implemented (P4).

## 1. Principles
- **Real data only.** Progress and awards are computed exclusively from real lifetime-ledger rows. Nothing is invented, sampled, pre-filled or estimated.
- **Lifetime is the source, not spendable.** Achievements read only `lifetime` ledger entries (at most one award per user/location/local day, by the G2 unique index). The spendable balance and cap never affect them, and spending (none exists) cannot reduce an achievement.
- **No streak pressure**, no loss-aversion timers, no public profiles or leaderboards.
- **No offers, merchants or money.** Any attached benefit would trigger the economics/legal review in `VIP_REWARDS_PLAN.md` section 6.
- **Reuse the architecture.** Postgres/Supabase functions, existing Expo/React Native screens and the existing `Icon` set. No new services, schedulers, licenses, fonts, art or photos.
- **Contributor badges are DEFERRED** (not in this specification and not in the catalog); approved-contribution data is not read.

## 2. Data sources and dependency map
Source: `reward_ledger` rows with `kind = 'lifetime'` (awards and reversals), joined to the visit's stored location, `policy_day_local` and `issuance_day_utc`. Held, confirmed-fraud and recorded-only visits contribute nothing; a held visit contributes after clearance, attributed to its ORIGINAL local date (G2 11.4a).
Dependencies: G2 (`record_visit`, ledger, unique lifetime index, reversals, held clearance, stored days, purge, reporting basis) -> G3 evaluation, event log and progress read -> dashboard progress card. Nothing in G3 feeds back into rewards eligibility.

## 3. Catalog: identity, versions and metrics (all proposals)
**Stable identity versus immutable versions.** Each achievement has a stable logical `achievement_key` (for example `explorer_month`). Definitions are immutable rows `(achievement_key, version)` with `effective_from_date` (a local date), metric, threshold, window rule and copy; a change inserts a new version row, never edits one. Events reference `(achievement_key, period_key)` as identity and record the `version` they were decided under, so a threshold change never creates a duplicate badge.
**Metrics, defined separately (no fixed conversions).** Point values are configurable, so no catalog text may say that N points equals M visits.
| Metric | Exact definition | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| `lifetime_points_total` | sum of lifetime ledger amounts (awards plus reversals) | all time |
| `distinct_locations` | count of distinct locations having at least one STANDING (non-reversed) lifetime award in scope: five check-in days at one restroom count 1 | all time or a window |
| `qualifying_awards` | count of non-reversed lifetime awards (each is one first-daily check-in at one location on one local day). Used ONLY by explicitly visit-count rules; it is NOT a count of different places | all time or a window |
| `distinct_active_days` | count of distinct location-local dates with at least one non-reversed lifetime award (several locations on one date count once) | a window |
Catalog proposals, each a (metric, copy) pair that must match: lifetime badges *First Stop* (`qualifying_awards` >= 1 all time; copy "Make your first check-in"), *Regular* (`lifetime_points_total` >= 50; copy "Reach 50 lifetime points"), *Century* (`lifetime_points_total` >= 100; copy "Reach 100 lifetime points"), *Trailblazer* (`distinct_locations` >= 10 all time; copy "Check in at 10 different restrooms"); monthly challenges *Explorer of the Month* (**window-scoped `distinct_locations` >= 5**; copy "Check in at 5 different restrooms this month") and *Steady Month* (`distinct_active_days` >= 8 in the month; copy "Check in on 8 different days this month"); one seasonal collectible (for example a winter season defined by two local dates, **window-scoped `distinct_locations`** >= 8; copy "Visit 8 different restrooms this winter"). No catalog row currently uses `qualifying_awards` in a window; it exists for an explicitly visit-count challenge if one is ever proposed, and its copy would then say "check-ins", never "places". Dormant by design: a distinct-towns badge until real data exists in more than one town. User value versus effort (candid): the value is acknowledgement and a small reason to return, and retention benefit is unvalidated; the cheapest validation is one lifetime badge plus one monthly challenge; effort is S/M for the catalog, evaluator and progress read, M for the dashboard card and M for seasonal content.
**Worked expected-result cases (also tests; point value = `lifetime_first_daily` = 5 unless stated).**
| Case | Inputs | Expected |
|---|---|---|
| Five days at one place | one restroom, five different local days in October, all awarded | Explorer 1 of 5 (`distinct_locations` = 1), no award; Steady Month 5 of 8 (`distinct_active_days` = 5); First Stop earned; `qualifying_awards` = 5; Regular 25 of 50 points |
| Five different places | five restrooms in October, one awarded day each | Explorer 5 of 5 -> `award`; Steady Month 5 of 8 if on five different days (1 of 8 if all on one day); Regular 25 of 50 |
| Configurable point value | `lifetime_first_daily` changed to 10 by a prospective version; five awards before it and five after | points total = 25 + 50 = 75: Regular earned at 75 of 50 under its own metric; copy still says "Reach 50 lifetime points" and never "10 visits" |
| Reversal removes one award at a place that has another | October: place A has awards on two days, B, C, D, E one each (6 awards, `distinct_locations` = 5, Explorer earned); one award at A is reversed | A still has a standing award -> `distinct_locations` stays 5 -> no `revoke`; `qualifying_awards` 5; points drop by 5 |
| Reversal removes the last standing award at a place | same user; the only award at E is reversed | E has no standing award -> `distinct_locations` = 4 -> Explorer `revoke` event referencing the reversal entry and decision; a later new award at a fifth place in the open window re-earns it |
| Reversal on a shared date | two awards on one local date at different places; one is reversed | `distinct_active_days` unchanged (the date still has a standing award) |

## 4. Version selection and historical windows (deterministic)
- **Windowed challenge or season:** the version used is the one with the greatest `effective_from_date` <= the window's `starts_on`, fixed when the window opens. A version never takes effect inside an open window, and historical windows are never silently re-judged by a newer threshold.
- **Lifetime badge (no window):** the version used for an award decision is the latest in effect at evaluation time. Once awarded under version v the award stands under v (the chain is then "awarded" and its version is the one recorded on the latest `award` event; progress reads and evaluation both call the SAME version-selection function): when a chain is "revoked" it holds no badge, so the next award decision and the displayed progress use the latest version in effect (prospective), never the version it was revoked under. Worked case: Regular v1 = 50 points, user awarded; v2 = 60 takes effect and the user still shows earned (v1); a reversal drops them to 45, below 50 under v1 -> `revoke`; they climb back to 52 and the progress shows 52 of 60 under v2, not earned; at 60 they are re-awarded under v2. While a chain is awarded under v, raising the threshold later never revokes it; recalculation after a reversal uses v, the version recorded on the award. Lowering a threshold applies prospectively: a user who meets the new lower threshold is awarded at the next evaluation after it takes effect (award date = that evaluation, never backdated).
- **Window membership** is the visit's stored location-local date, by the location's IANA zone, against the window's local `starts_on`/`ends_on`. A window closes for NEW visits when its end date passes, but the qualifying set of a closed window can still change through late clearance of a held visit or a reversal.
- **Single rule that unifies the cases:** after each evaluation the engine recomputes eligibility for each `(key, period_key)` from the current qualifying set under the selected version, and appends an event ONLY when that eligibility differs from the latest event's state. This one rule produces, deterministically and without duplicates:
  1. **first delayed award:** no prior event, the set now meets the threshold -> `award` (`reason = first_award`, `period_key` = the original window, `created_at` = now, shown as earned late);
  2. **recalculation or revocation of an old award:** latest event is `award`, the set no longer meets the threshold (for example a reversal) -> `revoke` referencing the reversal entry and its decision;
  3. **re-earning after revocation:** latest event is `revoke`, the set meets the threshold again through legitimate late events (a held visit cleared later, or visits inside an open window) -> a new `award` (`reason = re_earn`).
  There is no separate prohibition on closed windows: a closed window can only regain eligibility through legitimately delayed events dated inside it. Unresolved product behavior, stated plainly: whether a very late award (for example cleared weeks after the window) should be shown with an "earned late" label or suppressed from the headline view is a copy/content choice for later owner content review; the engine's default is to show it with the label.
- **Event sequence allocation:** `seq = max(seq) + 1` within one chain (`chain_id`, section 6), computed only while holding the per-user advisory lock (G2 lock order 1), with `unique (chain_id, seq)` as the backstop; every writer (visit, clearance, reversal, repair, purge) takes that same lock first. Chains are account-scoped: a chain belongs to exactly one `(user, achievement_key, period_key)`.
- **Worked scenarios (also tests):**
  - *Held October visit cleared in November.* Explorer of the Month v1 threshold 5. The user has 4 qualifying October places; the 5th visit on Oct 30 is held and cleared on Nov 3. Window October, version selected by window start (v1): the set becomes 5 -> one `award` event (`2026-10`, seq 1, v1, created Nov 3, "earned late"). The November window is unaffected (the visit counts on Oct 30 only).
  - *Reversal after month-end.* On Nov 10 an independent MFA admin reverses one of the five October lifetime awards. Recalculation for `2026-10` under v1 gives 4 -> one `revoke` event (seq 2) referencing the reversal entry and decision; the seq 1 award is untouched. If another held October visit is cleared on Nov 20 the set is 5 again -> `award` (seq 3, `re_earn`). Replaying the evaluation appends nothing.
  - *Catalog threshold change.* v2 (threshold 6) takes effect 2026-11-01. October keeps v1 (so the two scenarios above are unchanged); November uses v2. For the lifetime badge Regular, v1 = 50 and v2 = 60: a user awarded under v1 keeps it; a user at 55 who was not yet awarded sees 55 of 60 and gets nothing silently; lowering instead of raising awards prospectively as above.

## 5. Evaluation triggers, catch-up and freshness
- **Triggers.** `evaluate_achievements(user, scope)` runs INSIDE the transaction, before commit, never as an unspecified post-commit hook: after the reward in the visit transaction (a third, disjoint savepoint block A after blocks L and S, G2 section 4a), and at the end of the same transaction of every `award_held_visit` clearance and every `reverse_reward`, each in its own savepoint block. `scope` is the small set of `(achievement_key, period_key)` chains the triggering event can affect (the visit's local-date windows and lifetime badges), so per-event work is bounded by the catalog, not by history. Achievements are eventually consistent and can never block, roll back or alter a reward, a clearance or a reversal.
- **One lock order for every writer and repair (no queue-row/user-lock cycle).** The order is always: (1) the per-user advisory lock, then (2) that user's queue row and chain rows. Visit, clearance, reversal, evaluation, purge and repair all take the user lock BEFORE they lock, upsert, update or delete that user's `achievement_eval_queue` row, so no transaction can hold a queue row while waiting for the user lock.
- **Failure classes.** Expected, RECOVERABLE achievement failures are caught by an explicit SQLSTATE list (never `when others`): lock not available (`55P03`, only under an explicit local `lock_timeout`/NOWAIT) and a private `OSA01` for an invalid or missing catalog row. **This list is a HYPOTHESIS to be verified on real Postgres, not an established fact.** In particular `57014` (statement timeout or user cancellation) is NOT in the recoverable list: it cannot be told apart from a deliberate cancel, and a timeout caught inside a subtransaction does not re-arm for the rest of the outer statement, so it propagates until a real-Postgres test shows otherwise. Cancellation behavior, `40001`/`40P01` (serialization, deadlock: propagate) and the handler's interaction with `lock_timeout` are explicit items in the implementation test matrix; authorization and integrity failures are never hidden. Transaction-integrity and security failures PROPAGATE and abort the whole transaction: privilege errors (`42501`), integrity violations other than the designed `on conflict` no-ops (class `23`), data-corruption classes (`XX`), and any identity or authorization mismatch (private `OSA02`).
- **Logging survives the failed savepoint.** The exception handler runs in the outer transaction AFTER the inner block's writes are rolled back, still holding the user lock, and there it upserts `achievement_eval_queue(user_id primary key, reason, first_failed_at, attempts, last_sqlstate, dirty_scope)` (`dirty_scope` is the set of chains left unevaluated). The queue row commits with the visit, clearance or reversal; if the outer transaction itself fails, nothing persists, which is correct because the reward did not either.
- **Bounded idempotent repair, existing infrastructure only, no new scheduler or paid service.** (1) The authenticated `get_my_progress()` checks whether the caller has a queue row; if so it takes the caller's user lock FIRST, rechecks the row, evaluates a bounded slice, then updates or clears the row. (2) The admin-gated `repair_achievements(p_limit)` (existing admin identity and MFA, no adjudication power, no client-supplied amounts) works as follows: candidate selection is a plain NON-LOCKING read of up to `least(p_limit, 20)` oldest queue rows (the server maximum of 20 caps any caller-supplied `p_limit`); for each candidate it attempts the user lock with the NON-BLOCKING `pg_try_advisory_xact_lock`; if the attempt fails (a visit, reversal or another repair holds that user) the candidate is skipped and its row stays; if it succeeds it re-reads the queue row (now safely `for update`, because the user lock is already held), rechecks that it still exists and has dirty scope, and evaluates. Repair never waits for a user lock, so it cannot form a cycle with writers that wait. (3) **Per-user work is bounded as well:** one evaluation call processes at most 50 chains from `dirty_scope`, oldest window first, AND is also bounded by ledger rows scanned and elapsed time (values proposed in the implementation proposal and PROVEN on representative disposable data, not assumed); queue scope is MERGED (set union with the existing `dirty_scope`, capped, with an overflow flag that triggers a bounded full re-scan), never overwritten, and the queue row is deleted on account deletion so no user-linked repair data survives. If chains remain, the queue row is UPDATED with the remaining `dirty_scope` and `attempts + 1` and kept (unfinished work is retained, never dropped); the row is deleted only when `dirty_scope` is empty. One user alone is therefore not an unbounded cost, even with many windows of history. (4) Any external cron the project may already have is optional and out of scope.
- **Authoritative current eligibility and safe stale wording.** The progress read always computes `eligible_now` from the ledger and compares it with the latest event. It shows a badge as EARNED only when the latest event is `award` AND `eligible_now` is true. If the latest event is `award` but `eligible_now` is false (a reversal just happened), it shows "Being updated. This badge is under review and may change" and triggers repair; it never shows an ineligible badge as earned. If `eligible_now` is true but no award exists yet, it shows the progress as complete with "Updating" and triggers repair; it never shows an unearned badge as earned.
- **Scenarios (tests).** Failure then read/repair with no further visit (inject `55P03`, queue row exists, the visit and reward are committed, `get_my_progress()` repairs and clears the queue). Concurrent evaluation and reversal (both take the user lock; whichever is second sees the other's committed state; final state equals a single serial run; the reversal's own in-transaction evaluation (before its commit) produces the revoke). Replay (re-running the same evaluation appends no event). A propagating failure (forced `42501`) aborts the whole transaction and leaves no visit, award or event.

## 6. Authorization, provenance and deletion relationships
- **Internal vs public.** `evaluate_achievements(user)` and the queue repair helpers live in a schema not exposed through PostgREST, with `EXECUTE` revoked from `public`, `anon`, `authenticated` and `service_role`, callable only by the security-definer functions above. The only client read is `get_my_progress()`, which takes NO user parameter and derives identity from `auth.uid()`; for a signed-out caller it raises `28000`. All achievement tables have RLS enabled, no client grants and no client policies, so no client can read or write another user's rows or any row directly.
- **Adjudication authority is unchanged.** Revocation only reflects a ledger reversal already ordered through `reverse_reward` (existing independent MFA admin, no self-adjudication, immutable decision). The engine never adjudicates. `repair_achievements` is admin-gated and never changes ledger data.
- **Account-scoped chains with an opaque chain identity.** A chain is one account's history for one `(achievement_key, period_key)`. `achievement_chains(chain_id uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(), user_id, achievement_key, period_key)` with `unique (user_id, achievement_key, period_key)`; `chain_id` is random and opaque (never derived from the user id or a hash of it). `achievement_events(chain_id references achievement_chains ON DELETE RESTRICT, seq, event, version, reason, qualifying_value, threshold, basis_entry_id, decision_id, created_at)` with `unique (chain_id, seq)` and no `user_id` of its own. `basis_entry_id` and `decision_id` are set ONLY on `revoke` events (the reversal ledger entry and the moderation decision); `award` events store numeric snapshots and NO ledger or visit ids, so unmoderated awards depend on no personal history. FKs to the ledger and moderation tables are `ON DELETE RESTRICT` (never cascade), so a purge can never silently orphan or erase an event. Because key and period alone match every user, ALL closure logic is keyed by `chain_id` and the caller's `user_id`, never by key and period alone.
- **Retained dependency closure on account deletion (account-scoped).** For the deleted account, `chains` = rows with `chains.user_id = <caller>`. A chain is RETAINED iff it has at least one `revoke` event; a retained chain keeps all of its events (the revoke/award pairs and their sequence). Its dependencies are the reversal ledger entry (moderated, already retained by G2), its reversed original (retained by the G2 pair rule) and the moderation decision (never purged); nothing else is pulled in. Every other chain of that account, with its events, is DELETED, and no unrelated unmoderated history is retained. Retained chains get `chains.user_id` set to NULL (severed); `chain_id` stays unchanged, so the revoke/award relationships and `unique (chain_id, seq)` keep working and a severed chain can never match another user's chain. `chains.user_id` is `ON DELETE RESTRICT` to the auth user, so `delete_my_account` must run the purge first; a test asserts no chain or event still references the user. **What is retained is linkable audit data, not anonymous data:** minimized fields only (`chain_id`, `achievement_key`, `period_key`, and per event the `seq`, `event`, `version`, `reason`, numeric snapshot, `created_at`, and on revokes the reversal entry and decision ids); linkage to a person is possible through the retained moderation records. No legal-compliance claim.
- **Privileges, trigger and the purge-role check on EVERY mutation.** `reward_purge_owner` (G2 section 7) gets DELETE on `achievement_events` and `achievement_chains` and column UPDATE on `achievement_chains (user_id)` only; no UPDATE on events at all. Every client or non-owner role has no mutation privilege on either table. The invoker-security guard triggers (fixed `search_path`, `current_user`, never a setting) enforce: (a) any UPDATE or DELETE on events is refused unless `current_user = 'reward_purge_owner'`; events are otherwise immutable for everyone, the owner included except for deleting a non-retained chain's events; (b) a DELETE of a chain or its events is permitted only for `current_user = 'reward_purge_owner'` AND the chain has NO `revoke` event; (c) an UPDATE of `achievement_chains` is permitted only for `current_user = 'reward_purge_owner'` AND `user_id` goes from non-NULL to NULL AND every other column is unchanged AND the chain HAS a `revoke` event. A bare null transition by any other role is refused: the NULL update needs the purge-role check, not just the shape of the change.
- **Races.** The purge takes the per-user lock (order 1) before any queue, chain or ledger row lock, like evaluation, reversal, clearance and repair. Deletion racing evaluation or reversal ends in one of two states: they commit first and their new events are part of the chains the purge classifies after locking, or the purge commits first and the later call finds no user chains and is a no-op (a reversal of a deleted account's original is refused per G2). Repair's non-blocking attempt simply skips a user whose purge is in progress. A repeated purge is a no-op because severed chains have `user_id` NULL and match no caller.
- **Tests (expected outcomes).** Cross-user read/write refusal (another user's id as a parameter is impossible; direct table access refused for `anon` and `authenticated`); internal-function `EXECUTE` refusal; **two users sharing `explorer_month`/`2026-10` where only user A has a revoke:** A's chain is retained and severed (`chain_id` unchanged, award/revoke pair intact), B's chain and events are deleted, and neither purge touches the other user's chain; **repeated deletion after severing:** a second purge for A finds no chain with A's id and does nothing, and nothing matches another user; UPDATE-to-NULL by a non-purge role refused, by the purge role on a chain WITHOUT a revoke refused (it must be deleted instead), and by the purge role together with any other changed column refused; deletion racing reversal and evaluation; FK `RESTRICT` closure; severed rows keep only the minimized fields.

## 7. Windows and time zones
A window is a pair of LOCAL dates; a qualifying award counts when the visit's stored location-local date is in range, using the location's IANA zone (Cody: America/Denver), so a window never depends on the device clock or the user's own zone. A completed monthly challenge is awarded once per `period_key` (state changes follow section 4). Fixtures: last and first second of a month in America/Denver; DST days (2026-11-01 fall-back, 2027-03-14 spring-forward); a second zone; 23:59 local on the last day versus 00:01 the next (different windows even when the UTC date matches).

## 8. Accessible progress text, labels and states (proposals)
- **One progress element, spoken once.** Each challenge or badge with a numeric target is ONE element with `role="progressbar"` (React Native `accessibilityRole="progressbar"` with `accessibilityValue` min/max/now/text). Its accessible name is the achievement name; its value text is the full sentence, for example "3 of 5 places this month, 2 to go". The visible sentence is the same string drawn as text and is hidden from assistive technology (`aria-hidden` / `importantForAccessibility="no-hide-descendants"`) so it is not spoken twice; the visual bar is part of the same element, not a separate decoration claim. Meaning is never color-only; targets 48 px; fits 200 percent text and 320 px. Device behavior of this pattern must be checked with VoiceOver and TalkBack before acceptance (browser tests never count as reader evidence).
- **Badge cards:** name, state ("Earned 12 Oct 2026", "Earned late for October 2026", "Not earned yet"), and how to earn it written from the metric ("Reach 50 lifetime points", "Check in first-of-the-day at 5 different restrooms in a month"), with no visit/point conversion. A revoked badge says "Removed after a review" with no detail; a stale one says "Being updated. This badge is under review and may change."
- **States, distinguished truthfully.** (a) *Rewards disabled or not live:* keep the honest preview "VIP progress — rewards coming soon"; no numbers. (b) *Signed out:* "Sign in to track your progress." (c) *Enabled, signed in, evaluated:* real numbers, including a real zero ("0 of 5 places this month"), because the server computed it from the ledger for this user; enrollment is simply rewards being enabled plus an authenticated account, with no separate opt-in. (d) *Unavailable* (read error or evaluation not completed): "Progress is unavailable right now. Try again soon", never a zero. Zero is only ever shown in state (c); no sample data, no fake bars, no preview of badges the user cannot earn.
- A polite live region announces a newly earned badge; any celebration respects reduced motion and is optional. Placement: the dashboard VIP card becomes a compact progress card linking to a progress list in the existing account area; no new tab; no public history or other-user data.
