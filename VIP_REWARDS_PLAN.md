## 2. Approved rules (approved by Jake, recorded via Work 2026-10-11)
- **No cooldown and no other minimum interval between rewarded visits.** The earlier 60-minute cooldown proposal is rejected and superseded. Rapid genuine visits are allowed to receive all four rewards. Fraud holds are separate documented eligibility decisions (section 5), never a cooldown in disguise: a hold needs a recorded reason from section 5's signals, not elapsed time alone.
- Per user, per location, per **location-local calendar day** (Cody: America/Denver), rewarded visits are numbered in the order the server accepts them:
  | Visit that day at this location | Spendable | Lifetime |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1st | +5 | +5 |
  | 2nd, 3rd, 4th | +5 each | 0 |
  | 5th and later | 0 | 0 (the visit may still be recorded) |
- Maximum per user/location/local day: **20 spendable and 5 lifetime**. No seven-day declining schedule.
- **Account-wide spendable issuance cap, provisional: 60 spendable per user per UTC calendar day.** It is a SPENDABLE ISSUANCE cap, not a lifetime cap. Per-location counts and the first-daily rule use the location-local day.
- **Lifetime is modeled independently.** If a user's spendable capacity for the UTC day is exhausted, a visit that is the first of its local day at its location still earns its +5 lifetime. The first-daily lifetime rule is never silently changed by a spendable cap; the visit's outcome says so explicitly (section 11.3).
- Point values and limits are configurable and versioned; every change is prospective only.
- **Reversals:** an audited confirmed-fraud reversal may correct both spendable and lifetime balances as appropriate. **Gross issuance capacity is never restored by a reversal** (counters count points issued; section 4a).
- **Deletion (approved SUBJECT TO privacy/security review):** unmoderated rows are deleted and only the deleted totals are aggregated; moderated evidence is severed and preserved; the purge authority is a non-client-authorizable database role with no GUC bypass (section 7, review criteria in section 11.7).
- **Retention:** cleared fraud holds keep their flags for a provisional 90 days after clearance; confirmed-fraud audit follows the reviewed retention rule.
- **Optional GPS accuracy** is approved as a weak, client-reported, spoofable signal: it is never proof of presence and never used for fingerprinting.
- **Holds** are reviewed by the existing MFA admins, with no self-adjudication and an immutable audit trail. No new admin authority is created.
- Anonymous discovery is unchanged and requires no account.
- **Global production issuance budget: UNSET. Production rewards are DISABLED.** A local test fixture may set a budget on a disposable database to test behavior; no production amount is approved.
- Eligibility must not fabricate human presence: server trusted time, the existing auth and location evidence, idempotency, atomic concurrent cap enforcement.
- Points have no promised monetary value and nothing is redeemable now; premium or merchant offers need separate economics, cost, legal and integration approval.

## 3. The conflict and the replacement design
The 12-hour guard and the 10/day rate limit in today's `check_in` conflict with four rewarded visits per day. Do not just delete the guard. Split three concerns that are mixed in `check_in` today:
1. **Visit recording** (append-only `visits`): one row per accepted visit with server `occurred_at`, the location, a client-generated idempotency key (`unique (user_id, idempotency_key)`; a retry returns the same visit and never double-awards), and minimal evidence (a distance bucket computed server-side from the client coordinates, which are then discarded). Recording is always allowed within abuse limits.
2. **Reward eligibility** (a pure, versioned policy evaluation in the same transaction, section 11.2): the visit's ordinal at this location and local day (1-4 rewarded, 5+ recorded only), the user's spendable issuance for the UTC day against the cap, the global budget if one is set, and any hold. There is NO cooldown. The result and policy version are stored with the visit as **two independent outcomes**, a spendable outcome and a lifetime outcome (section 11.3).
3. **Monitoring thresholds, not eligibility gates**: 30 visits per user per day and 12 per user/location per day are NOT approved refusal thresholds. They are illustrative, configurable monitoring values for local planning only; exceeding one raises a flag for admin review and never refuses a visit, withholds an approved reward or imposes a hidden cap. Visits beyond the fifth at a location may simply be recorded. **Legacy limits are not inherited (Work routine decision):** future reward-capable recording is a NEW server path (working name `record_visit`) designed in the prospective G2 local design on a disposable database. It has NO 12-hour same-location guard and NO 10-per-day refusal as hidden eligibility restrictions, and G2 introduces no replacement refusal threshold. The live `check_in` function and its 12-hour and 10/day behavior stay exactly as they are until a separate, explicit authorization cuts over; nothing in G2 changes live behavior. Any future refusal limit needs its own concrete abuse/availability policy.

## 4. Ledger (design)
- `reward_policy_versions` (immutable): `id`, `effective_from`, `spendable_per_visit` (5), `lifetime_first_daily` (5), `max_rewarded_per_location_day` (4), `max_spendable_per_user_utc_day` (60, provisional), `max_spendable_global_utc_day` (NULL = unset: no issuance), `rewards_enabled` (default false), `created_by`, `created_at`. There is no cooldown column. Day bases are fixed by design (location-local for per-location rules, UTC for the cross-location and global caps), not configurable per row. A change inserts a new row; a trigger requires `effective_from > now()` so a change is always prospective.
- `reward_ledger` (append-only: UPDATE denied, and DELETE denied except the narrow deletion path in section 7):
  `id`, `entry_type` (`award` | `reversal`), `visit_id`, `user_id`, `policy_version`, `kind` (`spendable` | `lifetime`), `amount` (> 0 for an award, < 0 for a reversal), `reason`, `reverses_entry_id` (reversals only, references the original award row), `moderation_decision_id` (reversals only, references the admin decision that ordered it), `created_at`.
- **Awards and reversals are idempotent separately, so one never blocks the other.** Awards: a partial unique index `unique (visit_id, kind) where entry_type = 'award'`. Reversals: `unique (reverses_entry_id)` (an original row can be reversed at most once, whole amount only).
- **Cross-row rules are enforced by a function and a trigger, not by CHECK constraints** (a PostgreSQL CHECK cannot read another row). Only single-row rules are CHECKs: `(entry_type = 'award' and amount > 0 and reverses_entry_id is null and moderation_decision_id is null) or (entry_type = 'reversal' and amount < 0 and reverses_entry_id is not null and moderation_decision_id is not null)`. The ledger has no client grants, and rows are written only by security-definer functions:
  - `reverse_reward(p_entry uuid, p_decision uuid)` (admin-gated through the existing admin identity and MFA rules): `select ... from reward_ledger where id = p_entry for update` (the row lock serializes concurrent reversals of the same entry), then verifies in this order: the original exists and `entry_type = 'award'`; the referenced moderation decision exists, is a reward-reversal decision, is final, and was made by an admin other than the user concerned (no self-adjudication); then inserts the reversal copying `user_id`, `visit_id`, `kind` and `policy_version` from the ORIGINAL row and `amount = -original.amount` (so a mismatch is impossible by construction), with `on conflict (reverses_entry_id) do nothing`. When the conflict fires it returns the existing reversal row, so a repeated request cannot duplicate a reversal.
  - A `BEFORE INSERT` trigger repeats the same checks (original is an award, same user/kind/visit, exact negative amount, decision valid) under the same row lock, so a mistake in any future caller of the table is still refused.
  - **Concurrency tests (two real sessions):** two simultaneous `reverse_reward` calls for one entry yield exactly one reversal and two identical responses; a reversal racing the account-deletion path (section 7) ends in exactly one of two states: the reversal committed first, so the original and its reversal are both retained (severed, never deleted, never aggregated), or the purge committed first, so the original is gone or severed and the reversal is refused as "original not found or account deleted"; never half-applied, and in neither case is a moderated row deleted or counted in the deleted aggregate; a reversal attempted against another reversal, against a missing row, with a self-adjudicating admin, or with a non-reversal decision is refused; a direct INSERT by a client role is refused.
- **Balances are derived**: spendable = sum of spendable rows (awards plus reversals, minus future redemptions, none now); lifetime = sum of lifetime rows. `profiles.points_balance` becomes a cache written only by the award/reversal function and checked by a reconciliation query (cache vs ledger sum), part of the test suite and a periodic admin check. Clients never write any of it.
- All awards happen inside one security-definer function using server time; the client sends only the location, coordinates and an idempotency key.

### 4a. Concurrency: separate limits, separate serialization
The per-user/location/day advisory lock alone cannot enforce limits that span locations or users. Each limit needs its own atomically updated counter, taken in a fixed order so transactions cannot deadlock:
| Order | What is serialized | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Policy version in effect | read the immutable version row chosen by server `now()`; versions are insert-only and prospective, so no lock is needed and a transition cannot change a transaction midway |
| 1 | One user's visits | `pg_advisory_xact_lock(hash('user', user_id))` (stops one user racing themselves across locations) |
| 2 | User + location + local policy day (idempotency, visit ordinal, location cap, first-daily lifetime) | `pg_advisory_xact_lock(hash('uld', user_id, location_id, day))` then count ACCEPTED visits to assign the ordinal (the canonical ordinal is the accepted-visit count, never the rewarded count; a cap-blocked or held visit still occupies its slot) |
| 3 | User + policy day issuance (per-user daily cap) | `reward_counters_user_day(user_id, utc_day, spendable)` (SPENDABLE issuance only; there is no lifetime field and no reversal field) updated by `insert ... on conflict do update set spendable = spendable + :amount where spendable + :amount <= :cap returning` (an atomic conditional increment; zero rows returned means the cap is reached) |
| 4 | Global daily issuance (budget) | `reward_counters_global_day(day, spendable)` with the same conditional increment, always LAST because it is the hottest row; if contention appears, split it into N bucket rows, which changes only this step |
Lock order is always 0, 1, 2, 3, 4. **Two disjoint savepoint blocks, so a spendable failure can never erase an eligible lifetime award.** The award function runs, in one outer transaction: (a) idempotent visit insert and the evaluation; (b) **block L**, the lifetime award, in its own inner block: one ledger row of kind `lifetime`, guarded by a partial unique index `(user_id, location_id, policy_day_local) where kind = 'lifetime' and entry_type = 'award'` (so it is exactly one per user/location/local day even under races and even after a reversal); it touches no spendable counter; (c) **block S**, the spendable award, in a separate inner block: steps 3 and 4 (user-day counter, then the global counter) and the spendable ledger row. Each block is a PL/pgSQL `begin ... exception ... end`, i.e. its own implicit savepoint. If the user counter increments and the global increment returns zero rows, block S raises a private SQLSTATE and ITS writes (the user-counter increment and any spendable ledger row) are rolled back; block L's committed-in-transaction work is untouched, and the handler records `spendable_outcome = budget_exhausted` (or `user_day_cap` for a step-3 failure). **Transaction-wide integrity is still all-or-nothing:** the outer transaction commits the visit, both outcomes and whichever of L and S succeeded together, or, on any UNEXPECTED error, rolls back everything (no visit, no award) so the client retries with the same idempotency key. So budget is never consumed without a spendable award, a spendable award never exists without its counters, and a lifetime award is never lost to a spendable cap. A global budget that is UNSET or rewards disabled still issues nothing at all: the safety gate runs before blocks L and S, and `rewards_disabled` is recorded for both kinds.
**Examples (also tests).** *First visit, global budget exhausted:* the user's first visit of the day at location X; counters user 0/60, global at its limit. S fails with `budget_exhausted` and rolls back its user-counter increment; L stands. Result: exactly one lifetime +5 ledger row, no spendable row, user and global counters unchanged, visit recorded with `spendable_outcome = budget_exhausted`, `lifetime_outcome = awarded`. *User-cap race:* one user at 55/60 spendable makes first visits at locations X and Y in two sessions at once. The per-user lock (order 1) serializes them: whichever runs first takes the last 5 spendable (user counter reaches 60) and the other gets `user_day_cap`; BOTH get their +5 lifetime. Exactly one spendable award and two lifetime awards result, whichever session wins, and the user counter never exceeds 60. *Same-location double submit of the first visit with one idempotency key:* one visit, one lifetime row, one spendable row.
**Which day each counter uses.** Per user/location caps, the visit ordinal and the first-daily lifetime bonus use the **location-local policy day** (approved). The cross-location per-user cap and the global budget use **one UTC calendar day**, because they span locations and time zones and are budget/accounting limits, not user-facing "daily" promises. Consequence, stated plainly: a user visiting places in two time zones can receive up to the per-user cap per UTC day, and the local day and UTC day boundaries differ; both bases are recorded on the visit (`policy_day_local`, `issuance_day_utc`) so every counter can be reconciled. This basis is approved (section 9, row 9).
**Counters measure gross issuance, reversals do not reopen it.** The counters count points ISSUED. A reversal reduces the user's outstanding balance but does NOT decrement an issuance counter, so reversing fraud cannot reopen a day's budget and be farmed again. Reporting keeps three figures per UTC day: SPENDABLE issued (gross) comes from the counters (awards only); reversed and outstanding = issued - reversed (- redeemed, none now) are DERIVED from the ledger and the deleted aggregate. **No reversal counter and no lifetime counter exists.**
**Reporting and reconciliation basis (one rule for every figure).** Every issued and reversed figure is attributed to the **issuance day and month stored on the visit** (`issuance_day_utc`, and its UTC month), NEVER to the ledger row's `created_at`. That matters in two places: an award made later by `award_held_visit` clearance lands in the month of the original visit, and a reversal is attributed to the issuance month of the award it reverses (the date the reversal was ordered is kept only as informational audit data). The deleted-user aggregate (section 7) is keyed by the same stored issuance month. Reconciliation per UTC month for SPENDABLE: counters issued (summed over the month's days) = sum of awards whose visit's issuance month is that month (joined through the visit, not by `created_at`) + that month's deleted-aggregate issued. This counter reconciliation concerns spendable AWARDS only; reversed totals (spendable and lifetime) are ledger-derived plus the aggregate's reversed columns, with no counter to compare against.
**Lifetime issuance reporting (derived basis).** Lifetime awards touch no spendable counter and no cap, so there is no counter to reconcile against by design. Lifetime issued and reversed per UTC month are DERIVED from ledger rows of kind `lifetime` joined to the visit's stored issuance month, plus the deleted aggregate's lifetime columns; a reversal is attributed to its original award's month, so reversing never changes a month's issued figure. Honest limit: after deletion the figure is ledger plus aggregate, not an independent count; the independent checks that remain are per-user (the lifetime cache equals the ledger sum for live users) and the once-per-user/location/local-day unique index. If Work wants an independent control, an uncapped monthly accounting counter incremented inside block L is a possible addition (a hot row with no cap); it is not required by this design.
**Held visits and gross issuance.** A hold reserves NO capacity and releases none. Clearance (11.4a) may only consume capacity that remains on the visit's original UTC day under the stored policy's caps; it can never exceed a cap and never reopens or restores gross issuance. A rejected or `confirmed_fraud` held visit frees nothing.
**Policy transitions:** the policy day's counters carry across a change; the cap values used are those of the version in effect at the visit's server time, so lowering a cap applies to later visits that same day and never claws back earlier awards. Tests: two concurrent sessions at one location (cap), the same user at two locations (user cap), many users (global budget), retry with one idempotency key, a transition between two visits, the rollback test above.

## 5. Evidence limits and fraud signals (implementability and privacy)
Client coordinates do not prove presence: a client can send any coordinates, and the design never treats them as proof. Today's payload is `p_location`, `p_lat`, `p_lng` plus the authenticated user and server time. **Accuracy is not sent, coordinates are discarded after the 150 m check, and no device identifier or IP address is collected or planned.** No device fingerprinting is introduced.
| Signal | Inputs needed | Available today | Plan | Retention / privacy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rapid repeat visits | user, location, server time | yes (visit rows) | **informational only: NEVER a hold, refusal, cooldown or ineligibility.** Rapid same-location visits are genuine and approved up to four rewards; the fifth is recorded-only through the ordinal rule, not as a suspicion | the visit row (user, location, time) lives until account deletion (section 7) |
| Cap and rate-limit hits | counters, `action_log`, reason on the visit | yes | implement in G2 | counter rows kept as aggregates; per-user detail with the visits |
| Retry duplicates | idempotency key | no, new argument | implement in G2 (additive, optional) | key stored with the visit |
| Distance bucket from the restroom | client coordinates, restroom coordinates | partly (checked, then discarded) | store only the bucket (for example <= 50 m, 50-150 m), never the coordinates | bucket only; weak, claim-derived evidence |
| Implausible movement between visits | the RESTROOM coordinates of two consecutive visits at DIFFERENT locations and the server times | yes, without storing any user coordinate | implement in G2: distance between the two restrooms divided by elapsed server time above a plausible travel speed flags `held`; same-location visits never produce a movement signal, so rapid repeats can never trigger it | derived from public location data and server time; weak, because both visits are still claims |
| Accuracy ceiling | client-reported accuracy | **no** (not in the payload) | approved as an optional weak signal (G2 adds the optional argument; the client change waits for its own package); client-reported and spoofable, never proof of presence; a missing value is NOT suspicious by itself | accuracy bucket only |
| Multi-account or shared-device patterns | device or network identifiers | **no** | **defer**; needs legal and privacy review; no fingerprinting; the only conceivable first-party signal (accounts with near-identical visit sequences) is not built without review | none collected |
**Suspicious is not fraud.** States: `eligible` -> `held` (reversible, reason recorded) -> `cleared` or `confirmed_fraud` (reversing ledger rows, section 4). Every change goes through the existing admin identity, MFA and `moderation_log` audit pattern; no self-adjudication; no automatic punitive account action without human review. The existing Phase 3A rules (immutable originals, append-only decisions, self-adjudication block, provenance kept with the person severed) are preserved. Retention of the per-visit flags and hold reasons: provisional 90 days after a hold is cleared (approved as provisional); confirmed-fraud audit records follow section 7 and the reviewed retention rule.

## 6. Economics model (planning, not an accounting or legal conclusion)
- Spendable issuance = 5 x eligible visits. Per user/location/day exposure is at most 20 spendable (and 5 lifetime); with the provisional per-user cap of 60 spendable per UTC day and a global daily budget (unset in production), SPENDABLE issuance per user per day is bounded no matter how many places are visited. **Lifetime is not bounded by those caps** (by design it is independent): it is bounded only by one +5 per user/location/local day, the abuse limits and the implausible-movement hold, so lifetime exposure grows with distinct locations visited. Lifetime is non-spendable and attaches no benefit, which is why this is acceptable for now; any benefit attached to it triggers the review in the next paragraph.
- **What is true today:** no redeemable reward, cash-out, price or promise exists in the product, and nothing issues points. **What is not known:** points that users can earn create expectations, and unpriced future exposure can arise before any redemption exists (promotional commitments, accounting treatment, consumer-protection or loyalty-program rules). The plan makes no claim that exposure is zero; it is unpriced and unassessed. Legal and accounting review is required before any points are issued.
- **Lifetime points** are designed as an achievement counter, not spendable. If any benefit, tier, discount or access is ever attached to them they stop being purely cosmetic and need the same review; until then no benefit attaches.
- Illustration only, not a forecast: 1,000 active users averaging 2 rewarded visits a day issue 10,000 spendable points a day, 300,000 a month. At a hypothetical redemption cost of $0.002 per point and full redemption that would be $600 a month. Real numbers need a priced redemption and a measured redemption rate.
- Controls: per-user and global daily issuance caps, a budget switch that pauses issuance prospectively, versioned configuration, and user-facing copy that says points have no cash value.

## 7. Privacy and deletion (resolving the append-only conflict)
The ledger is append-only (no UPDATE, no DELETE), but deleting an account must remove the person's data, and today `checkins` cascade on account deletion. These conflict; the alternatives:
- **A. Sever and keep:** keep ledger and visit rows, set `user_id` to null at deletion. Needs a narrow carve-out in the immutability trigger (only `user_id: value -> null`, only inside the deletion function) and leaves granular events (location, time, amounts) with no user. Reconciliation stays exact. Re-identification risk is small but real for sparse data (one user visiting a rarely used restroom at a known time).
- **B. Delete and aggregate (recommended):** deletion is performed by one narrow function, `purge_reward_data_for_deleted_user(p_user uuid)`, called only from the existing `delete_my_account` (security definer) path. **Authorization is by database role, never by a session setting or GUC:** the function is owned by a dedicated non-login role (`reward_purge_owner`) that no client role is a member of and cannot `SET ROLE` to, EXECUTE on it is revoked from `public`, `anon` and `authenticated` (only `delete_my_account` reaches it), and the append-only trigger permits DELETE (and the narrow `user_id -> null` update below) only when `current_user = 'reward_purge_owner'`. `current_user` inside a security-definer function is the function owner and cannot be set by a caller, unlike `set_config`. **Trigger and role specification:** the guard trigger function is `SECURITY INVOKER` (the default), deliberately NOT `SECURITY DEFINER`: a definer trigger would run as its own owner and `current_user` would no longer show the real caller, hiding exactly the role being checked. Inside the purge function the invoker is `reward_purge_owner`, so the check passes only on that path. The check uses `current_user`, never `session_user` (the login role) and never a setting. Both functions fix `set search_path = pg_catalog, pg_temp` and schema-qualify every object (`public.reward_ledger`), so no caller can shadow a table or function. `reward_purge_owner` is `NOLOGIN NOINHERIT NOSUPERUSER NOCREATEDB NOCREATEROLE NOREPLICATION NOBYPASSRLS`; no role is a member of it (the migration role is granted membership only to run `ALTER FUNCTION ... OWNER TO` and is revoked in the same migration, so the final state has no members). It holds only what the purge needs: SELECT and DELETE on the visit and ledger tables, `UPDATE (user_id)` on them, INSERT/UPDATE on `reward_deleted_aggregate`, SELECT on the moderation tables, plus RLS policies `to reward_purge_owner` only. The purge function lives in a schema that is not exposed through PostgREST, `EXECUTE` is revoked from `public`, `anon`, `authenticated` and `service_role` (Supabase's default function privileges would otherwise grant it) and granted only to the owner role of `delete_my_account`. No client role can acquire purge authority: none is a member, none can `SET ROLE` to it, none can execute the function, and the trigger ignores every setting. A freely settable flag (the bypass Phase 3A removed) is explicitly NOT used. The function takes the user from `auth.uid()` through `delete_my_account`, never from a client argument, so it cannot be pointed at another person.
  - **Retained and deleted rows are two disjoint partitions, and only the deleted partition is aggregated.** In one transaction the purge (1) takes row locks on all of the user's visit and ledger rows in a fixed order (`order by id for update`), so a racing reversal either finished before the locks or waits; (2) computes the **retained set R** AFTER the locks: every row referenced by a hold, a moderation decision or a confirmed-fraud record, plus its reversal pair (an award and the reversal that references it are retained together, so no orphan reversal and no award whose reversal was kept without it); (3) runs `with d as (delete from reward_ledger where user_id = <caller> and id <> all(R) returning ...) insert into reward_deleted_aggregate ... select ... from d` (and the same for visit rows), so the aggregate is built from the rows the DELETE actually returned, never from a separate query that could disagree; (4) severs R (`user_id -> null`). Aggregate rows carry no identifier and are keyed by UTC MONTH (minimum fields in 11.7): `reward_deleted_aggregate(utc_month, issued_spendable, issued_lifetime, reversed_spendable, reversed_lifetime, visit_count)`. Retained rows are NOT in the aggregate because they stay in the ledger with their original amounts.
  - **Worked example (also a test fixture).** One user, all +10: award A1 (moderated, later reversed by R1 = -10), award A2 (no moderation), award A3 (reversed later by R2 = -10, a moderated reversal), award A4 (no moderation). Retained R = {A1, R1, A3, R2}; deleted D = {A2, A4}. Aggregate gains issued +20 and reversed 0 (plus the two deleted visits). The ledger keeps A1, A3 (issued 20) and R1, R2 (reversed 20) with `user_id` null. Check: gross issuance counters (A1 to A4 = 40) = ledger issued (20) + aggregate issued (20); reversal counter (20) = ledger reversed (20) + aggregate reversed (0). A user with no moderated rows: everything is in D, the ledger has none of their rows and the aggregate gains all of it. A user whose every row is moderated: aggregate gains nothing and every row is severed.
  - **Moderated evidence is preserved unconditionally.** Any visit or ledger row referenced by a hold, a moderation decision or a confirmed-fraud record is NOT deleted: the trigger lets the purge role only set its `user_id` to null (severing the person, the Phase 3A principle), and the moderation tables are never touched by the purge. Everything else for that user is deleted.
  - **Severing is column-scoped, not just `UPDATE (user_id)`.** `reward_purge_owner` is granted column-level UPDATE only on: ledger `(user_id)`; visits `(user_id, distance_bucket, accuracy_bucket, idempotency_key)`. It has no UPDATE on amounts, kinds, `entry_type`, `occurred_at`, `location_id`, outcomes, policy version, days, reasons or decision references. The invoker-security guard trigger additionally validates each permitted UPDATE narrowly: for the allowed columns the new value must be NULL (cleared), and every other column must equal the old value (compared as the row minus the allowed columns), otherwise the statement is refused. **Audit-original preservation is unconditional:** the fields the audit needs (visit id, location id, `occurred_at`, outcomes, amounts, decision reference) are never altered by the purge, and a severing step that would change any of them fails and rolls the whole purge back. `unique (user_id, idempotency_key)` tolerates NULLs, so clearing the key does not collide.
  - **Authorization tests:** `authenticated` and `anon` cannot DELETE or UPDATE ledger/visit rows even after `set_config('os.account_deletion', 'on', true)`; cannot EXECUTE the purge function; cannot `SET ROLE reward_purge_owner`; the purge cannot be aimed at another user; role evidence queries must hold: `reward_purge_owner` has the attributes above in `pg_roles`, `pg_auth_members` has no row naming it as the granted role, `has_function_privilege` is false for `anon`, `authenticated` and `service_role`, and the guard trigger function has `prosecdef = false` and a fixed `search_path` in `proconfig`; a DELETE by the migration role or any non-owner role is refused by the trigger too; the purge owner cannot UPDATE any column outside the allowed list (column-privilege check in `information_schema.column_privileges`) and a crafted UPDATE that clears a bucket while also changing an amount, `occurred_at` or `location_id` is refused by the trigger; the worked example above produces exactly the stated aggregate (issued +20, reversed 0), the retained and deleted partitions are disjoint and together equal the user's rows, and a second purge for the same user is a no-op; a purge removes the user's unmoderated rows, writes exactly the matching aggregate, leaves moderated rows severed rather than deleted, and the reconciliation identity of section 4a still holds afterwards; a purge racing a reversal is safe (section 4).
  Reconciliation compares cache and ledger sums per user for live users and, globally, ledger (including severed retained rows) + deleted aggregate = counters on the section 4a basis. The moderation records remain in the existing moderation tables, not the ledger.
- **C. Pseudonymize (keep a salted hash of the user id): rejected.** Pseudonymized data is still personal data.
Policy implications for the owner: under B a user's points and history disappear with the account (said plainly in the deletion screen before any points exist), exact historical per-user economics cannot be reconstructed after deletion, and fraud cases remain auditable. Coordinates are never stored under any option. This changes no approved Phase 3A deletion or provenance rule; it extends the same rule to the new tables.

## 11. G2 implementation specification and acceptance/test plan (planning only)
Scope when authorized: local-only source migrations, SQL functions and tests on a **disposable local Postgres**. Not in scope: any live database, deployment, UI, rewards display, activation, merchant redemption, photos, or a global production budget. Production keeps `rewards_enabled = false`.

### 11.1 Server-only
All evaluation and awarding happens in the database in one security-definer function. The client sends only the location, its coordinates (checked against 150 m, then discarded), an optional accuracy value and an idempotency key. The client can never submit an amount, a day, a count or an outcome, and no TypeScript in the mobile app computes eligibility or balances. A TypeScript reference model may exist in test code only, as an oracle for cross-checking the SQL; it is never imported by the app.

### 11.2 Pure evaluation
`evaluate_visit(policy, facts) -> outcome` is a pure SQL function (no table access, no `now()`): inputs are the policy version row, the visit's ordinal among accepted visits that user/location/local day, the user's spendable already issued that UTC day, the global issued that UTC day, and the hold flag. The award function gathers the facts under the section 4a locks and then applies `evaluate_visit`, so the rules are testable by table-driven unit tests without a database state.
**Ordinal.** The ordinal counts every accepted, non-rejected visit in server-acceptance order, whether or not it earned points; a visit whose spendable is blocked by a cap still occupies its slot (otherwise a cap hit would let a later visit jump into a slot).

### 11.3 Two independent outcomes per visit
`spendable_outcome`: `awarded` (+5) | `location_cap` (ordinal 5+) | `user_day_cap` | `budget_exhausted` | `budget_unset` | `rewards_disabled` | `held`.
`lifetime_outcome`: `awarded` (+5, ordinal 1 only) | `not_first_daily` | `rewards_disabled` | `held`.
They are decided separately: a first-of-day visit whose spendable is `user_day_cap` has `lifetime_outcome = awarded`. A user-facing result must name the actual outcome (no silent drop and no fabricated earned state). Awards are all-or-nothing per entry; with 5-point steps and a 60 cap no partial grant arises, and a future non-dividing policy is refused as capped rather than partially granted.
Decision table (cap 60, per-location 4; all values +5 per spendable award):
| Situation | Spendable | Lifetime |
|---|---|---|
| 1st visit of the local day at a location, caps free | awarded | awarded |
| 2nd-4th visit | awarded | not_first_daily |
| 5th visit, 6th, ... | location_cap (visit recorded) | not_first_daily |
| 1st visit, user already at 60 spendable this UTC day | user_day_cap | awarded |
| 3rd visit, user at 60 | user_day_cap | not_first_daily |
| Global budget set and exhausted | budget_exhausted | per rule above (independent of spendable) |
| Global budget unset or rewards disabled | budget_unset / rewards_disabled | rewards_disabled (nothing is issued; the visit is recorded) |
| Visit held | held (no award yet) | held |
Rapid repeats are allowed: four visits in four minutes at one location earn 20 spendable and 5 lifetime; the fifth is recorded only.

### 11.4 Ledger, counters, locking, rollback
As sections 4 and 4a: append-only ledger with `entry_type` award/reversal; separate idempotency for awards (`unique (visit_id, kind) where entry_type='award'`) and reversals (`unique (reverses_entry_id)`); the lifetime once-per-user/location/local-day index of section 4a; atomic conditional counters for the user UTC day and (when a budget is set) the global UTC day; fixed lock order 0-4; **two disjoint inner blocks** (lifetime L, spendable S): a failed S leaves no spendable counter or ledger residue and never removes an eligible lifetime award, and the whole visit commits or rolls back as one outer transaction (section 4a). There is no cooldown. **Held visits** keep their slot and award nothing. **`award_held_visit` (admin clearance)** is specified in 11.4a.

#### 11.4a `award_held_visit`
Inputs: the visit id and an immutable admin decision (existing admin identity and MFA, decided by an admin other than the user; `decided_at` is the server time of clearance). It uses ONLY values frozen on the visit at acceptance: the **original policy version**, the original `policy_day_local` and `issuance_day_utc`, and the ORDINAL assigned (and locked) at acceptance. It never reads today's policy, never recomputes the ordinal, and evaluates no time interval, so clearance can neither apply a cooldown nor reset or reject on one. Steps: lock in section 4a order using the visit's ORIGINAL days; run block L (lifetime, if ordinal 1) and block S (spendable, if ordinal <= 4) separately with the stored version's caps against the original UTC-day counters; record each outcome as an appended, immutable outcome event (the original `held` outcome row is never edited). Because L and S are disjoint, an S failure at clearance (`user_day_cap` or `budget_exhausted`, capacity used by later visits that day) never erases the eligible lifetime award, and that rejected spendable outcome is final (no automatic retry). Idempotency: `unique (visit_id) where decision = 'clear'` plus a row lock on the visit and the existing per-kind award uniqueness; a repeated or racing clearance returns the existing outcomes and creates no second award or decision. **Historical clearance never reopens gross issuance** (see section 4a: a hold reserves no capacity; clearance only consumes what remains on the original day). **No backdating:** award rows carry `created_at` = clearance time and the decision's own server `decided_at`; reconciliation by issuance day uses the visit's stored days, never the clearance date. **Policy changes while held:** a newer policy version never applies to the held visit (versions are prospective), so lowering or raising a cap in the meantime does not change what clearance may award. `confirmed_fraud` awards nothing and the hold stays as audit. Tests: clearance after the version was superseded uses the old version; clearance after later visits exhausted the day's cap gives `user_day_cap` while still awarding the first-visit lifetime; two simultaneous clearances yield one award set; a self-adjudicating admin is refused; clearance with no elapsed-time input succeeds immediately (no cooldown).

### 11.5 Policy versions, time zones, DST
Versions are immutable and prospective (`effective_from > now()` enforced). The version applies by the visit's server time. Local day = `(occurred_at at time zone location.tz)::date` using the location's IANA zone; the UTC day is `(occurred_at at time zone 'UTC')::date`; both are stored on the visit. A "day" is a calendar date, so a DST local day may be 23 or 25 hours long and nothing assumes 24 hours.
Boundary fixtures (America/Denver): the last second before and the first second after local midnight start different per-location days; UTC midnight falls at 18:00 MDT (UTC-6) or 17:00 MST (UTC-7), so one local day spans two UTC days and the 60 cap applies to each UTC day separately (reaching 60 needs at least three locations, since one location yields at most 20 per local day, so 120 across one local evening needs six distinct locations: a UTC-cap consequence the owner approved); spring-forward 2027-03-14 (23-hour local day) and fall-back 2026-11-01 (25-hour local day); a second fixture zone (for example Pacific/Honolulu or Asia/Kolkata) proves the per-location rule uses the location's zone and the cap uses UTC; a location with a missing/invalid zone fails closed (recorded, no award).

### 11.6 Optional accuracy, conservative holds
An optional accuracy value is stored only as a bucket and never changes the 150 m check. Missing accuracy is normal and never suspicious. Poor accuracy alone never denies a genuine visit: it can contribute to a `held` outcome only together with another signal (implausible movement, abuse limit). Held reasons are recorded and reviewed (section 5); a visit that is recorded-only or held never shows or creates earned state.

### 11.7 Deletion: privacy/security review criteria
Before the purge is implemented Work/privacy review must confirm the following. **Minimum anonymous aggregate (specified):** `reward_deleted_aggregate(utc_month, issued_spendable, issued_lifetime, reversed_spendable, reversed_lifetime, visit_count)` and nothing else: no user, account, cohort, session, device or IP identifier; no location id or coordinates; no time of day; no policy-version or per-user count. It is keyed by **UTC month**, not day, as a minimization choice (day-level rows on a quiet day could equal a single person's totals); the reconciliation identity of section 4a is checked at month granularity (counters summed over the month = ledger awards in the month plus the month's aggregate). The table has no client grants, no RLS policy for client roles and no client-callable reader; admins see only an admin-gated reporting function that returns monthly totals. Low-count months are possible in a small market; the residual risk is acknowledged and stated, not claimed away. **Retention limitation:** the aggregate holds no personal identifiers and is kept as accounting data; severed moderated rows keep only what the audit needs (visit id, location id, `occurred_at`, outcomes, amounts, decision reference), with the distance bucket, accuracy bucket and idempotency key cleared at severing, and are kept only for the retention period the reviewed rule sets (confirmed-fraud audit follows the Phase 3A rule; this plan does not invent a number). **Terminology:** a severed row with detailed location and time is not anonymous, it is de-identified-but-linkable data that can be re-identified from outside knowledge (for example one person at a rarely used restroom at a known time); this plan does not call it anonymous and claims no legal compliance. The minimum audit required by the nine approved Phase 3A decisions (provenance kept with the person severed) is preserved. Also confirm, by cross-reference rather than restating: the authorization, role and trigger criteria and the role evidence queries (section 7, authorization tests); the disjoint partition, returned-rows aggregate and worked example (section 7); column-scoped severing and unconditional audit-original preservation (section 7); the row-lock order and the reversal-versus-purge race (sections 4 and 7); idempotency of a repeated purge (test 7 in 11.9); and that the 90-day cleared-hold flag removal is a documented routine (the local mechanism is a callable function; any scheduler such as an extension or cron is a separate approval because it may add cost or infrastructure). These are review constraints for Work, not a privacy or legal clearance and not an owner decision.

### 11.8 Existing MFA audit integration (no authority expansion)
Holds and reversals use the existing admin identity, MFA gate, `moderation_log` and decision records. No new role, no new admin power: `reverse_reward` and the hold decisions require the same independent-admin checks as Phase 3A, with no self-adjudication; each decision is immutable and audited.

### 11.9 Acceptance and test plan (all on a disposable local Postgres, plus pure table-driven unit tests)
1. **Policy table-driven tests** for every row of the 11.3 decision table, including independence of lifetime from the spendable cap.
2. **Rapid genuine visits (no cooldown)**: four visits within a minute at one location earn exactly 20 spendable and 5 lifetime with no hold and no rejection; the fifth is recorded-only with `location_cap`, also with no hold; a table-driven assertion shows that no code path computes an interval between same-location visits.
3. **UTC cap across local days and time zones**: the same user across two local days straddling UTC midnight, and across two zones; the cap counts per UTC day only.
4. **Retries and races**: one idempotency key from two sessions yields one visit and one set of awards; two sessions racing for the 4th slot and for the 60th spendable point each yield exactly one award; the lock-order test shows no deadlock across locations.
5. **Rollback and independence**: forced global exhaustion on a FIRST visit leaves user and global counters unchanged, no spendable row, exactly one lifetime +5 row, and the visit recorded with `budget_exhausted` and `lifetime_outcome = awarded`; the user-cap race (user at 55/60, first visits at two locations) gives one spendable and two lifetime awards in either order and the counter never exceeds 60; an unexpected error rolls back the whole visit (no partial state) and a retry with the same key succeeds; with budget unset or rewards disabled neither kind is issued.
6. **Balances and reversals**: balances equal ledger sums; a reversal corrects spendable and lifetime but the issuance counters stay gross; concurrent duplicate reversals produce one; self-adjudication and non-reversal decisions are refused.
7. **Retention and deletion**: the worked example of section 7 (aggregate carries only the minimum monthly fields, month-granularity reconciliation holds, severed rows have bucket/accuracy/idempotency fields cleared); disjoint aggregates; moderated rows severed; second purge a no-op; role evidence queries; reversal-versus-purge race; cleared-hold flags older than 90 days removed, newer kept.
8. **Policy transitions and DST** per 11.5; prospective-only versions. **Reporting basis:** a held-visit award cleared in a later month is reported in the visit's issuance month (not the clearance month); a reversal is attributed to its original award's month; spendable month reconciliation holds with deleted aggregates; lifetime monthly derived totals match ledger plus aggregate and reversals never change a month's issued figure; the canonical ordinal equals the accepted-visit count (a cap-blocked visit occupies its slot, a held visit keeps its slot); the legacy `check_in` behavior is unchanged by the new `record_visit` path on the disposable database.
9. **Held-visit clearance**: the 11.4a tests (original policy version, locked ordinal and original days; independent L/S; repeated and racing clearance; no backdating; no self-adjudication; no elapsed-time input).
10. **Safety**: `rewards_enabled = false` and a NULL global budget issue nothing; no client grants on any new table; the client cannot supply an amount or day; no coordinates stored; no device identifiers collected; anonymous discovery unaffected; `npm run check` and the existing e2e suites unchanged.
Evidence format: a short result table per item and the exact commit; no device, reader or production claims.
